How to Save Money on Gas: 10 Practical Tips for Every Driver

Fuel is one of the regular expenses that can make owning a car expensive. Whether you drive to work every day, travel frequently, or use your car for family errands, the amount you spend on gas can add up quickly.

The good news is that you don't always need to buy a more fuel-efficient car to reduce your fuel expenses. Simple changes to your driving habits, regular car maintenance, and smarter travel planning can help you use less fuel.

You can also consider carpooling with people who travel along the same route. Instead of driving alone and paying for the entire journey yourself, sharing a ride can help divide fuel costs among passengers.

Here are 10 practical ways to save money on gas without making your daily travel unnecessarily difficult.

## 1. Keep Your Car Tires at the Recommended Pressure

One of the simplest ways to improve fuel efficiency is to check your tire pressure regularly.

When tires are underinflated, they create more rolling resistance. This means your car needs more energy to move, which can increase fuel consumption.

Make it a habit to check your tire pressure at least once a month and before long journeys.

**What you should do:**

- Check the recommended pressure in your vehicle's manual or on the manufacturer's tire-pressure label.
- Check tire pressure when the tires are cold, where possible.
- Inspect your tires regularly for leaks, damage, or uneven wear.
- Keep the spare tire properly inflated if your vehicle has one.

Do not overinflate your tires in an attempt to save fuel. Always follow the vehicle manufacturer's recommended pressure.

Correct tire pressure can improve fuel efficiency, extend tire life, and support safer handling.

## 2. Remove Unnecessary Weight From Your Car

Do you keep old bags, tools, sports equipment, or other heavy items in your car even when you don't need them?

Extra weight makes your vehicle work harder, particularly during acceleration. The effect can be more noticeable in smaller and lighter cars.

Take a few minutes to clean your trunk and remove items that you don't need for your journey.

For example, you could remove:

- Heavy bags that you rarely use.
- Unnecessary tools or equipment.
- Boxes and other unused items.
- Roof cargo boxes and racks when they are not needed.

You don't need to remove essential safety equipment, a spare tire, or anything required for your journey. The goal is simply to avoid carrying unnecessary weight.

## 3. Avoid Unnecessary Braking and Sudden Acceleration

Another way to save fuel is to drive smoothly rather than repeatedly accelerating quickly and braking hard.

Imagine driving toward a traffic signal. If you notice the signal changing from a distance, you can ease off the accelerator and slow down gradually when conditions allow. Accelerating toward the signal only to brake suddenly wastes energy.

Try to:

- Accelerate gradually.
- Maintain a safe following distance.
- Look ahead for traffic signals, congestion, and slow-moving vehicles.
- Keep a steady speed when traffic and road conditions permit.
- Slow down smoothly when approaching a stop.

This doesn't mean you should avoid using your brakes when necessary. **Safety always comes first.** Brake whenever needed to maintain a safe distance, obey traffic rules, or avoid a collision.

The idea is to anticipate traffic so you can avoid unnecessary acceleration and hard braking.

## 4. Use Carpooling to Share Fuel Costs

One of the most effective ways to reduce your personal fuel expenses is to avoid making every journey alone.

Suppose you drive to work five days a week and two colleagues live near your route. If all three of you travel in one car, you may be able to share the fuel expense rather than paying the full cost yourself.

Here's a simple example.

| Travel arrangement | Total fuel cost per trip | Your share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Driving alone | $15 | $15 |
| Sharing with one other passenger | $15 | $7.50 |
| Sharing with two other passengers | $15 | $5 |

*Illustrative example only. These figures assume the same total fuel cost, three equal shares at most, and no additional detours or other costs.*

Your actual savings depend on the route, distance, vehicle, fuel price, and how costs are divided.

Carpooling can work well for:

- Daily office commutes.
- College journeys.
- Regular trips to nearby cities.
- Events and concerts.
- Weekend trips with friends.
- Airport journeys.

## 5. Combine Multiple Errands Into One Trip

Do you drive to the supermarket in the morning, return home, and then drive out again to collect a parcel or buy something else?

Making several separate trips can increase fuel consumption, particularly when each journey begins with a cold engine.

Instead, plan your errands so that nearby destinations can be visited in one outing.

For example, you could:

1. Make a shopping list before leaving home.
2. Group nearby stops together.
3. Choose a route that avoids unnecessary backtracking.
4. Check traffic conditions before starting.
5. Complete several errands during one trip where practical.

This approach can save fuel, reduce driving time, and limit unnecessary wear on your vehicle.

## 6. Avoid Excessive Idling

Idling means leaving your engine running while the vehicle is stationary.

For example, you might leave your car running while waiting for someone outside a shop or while parked for an extended period.

During that time, the engine consumes fuel without moving the vehicle.

Whenever it is safe and appropriate, switch off the engine during longer stops. Follow your vehicle's instructions and local regulations, and never switch off the engine while moving.

If your car has an automatic stop-start system, using it as intended can help reduce unnecessary idling.

Avoid letting the engine run for long periods just to warm it up. Most modern cars do not require extended stationary warm-ups; follow the manufacturer's guidance, especially in very cold conditions.

## 7. Maintain a Sensible and Consistent Speed

Driving at high speeds can increase fuel consumption because air resistance rises as speed increases.

On highways, try to maintain a steady, legal speed instead of repeatedly speeding up and slowing down.

Here are some useful habits:

- Follow posted speed limits.
- Accelerate smoothly when entering faster-moving traffic.
- Use cruise control on suitable highways if your vehicle has it and conditions permit.
- Slow down when traffic, weather, or road conditions require it.
- Avoid unnecessary speeding and rapid lane changes.

Cruise control is not suitable for every road or weather condition, so use it only when safe.

The aim is not to drive unusually slowly. It is to travel at an appropriate speed without wasting fuel through unnecessary acceleration.

## 8. Remove Roof Racks and Cargo Boxes When Not Needed

Roof racks, bike carriers, and cargo boxes can increase wind resistance, particularly at higher speeds.

Even when a roof rack is empty, it may create additional drag. That means your vehicle can use more fuel to maintain the same speed.

If you no longer need a roof rack or cargo box, consider removing it according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Also, avoid loading heavy luggage onto the roof unless necessary. Carrying suitable luggage inside the vehicle may reduce aerodynamic drag, provided it is securely stored and does not obstruct the driver's view or compromise passenger safety.

These small changes can help improve fuel efficiency on longer journeys.

## 9. Keep Your Car Properly Maintained

Poor vehicle maintenance can reduce fuel efficiency and increase repair costs.

Regular servicing helps your car run as intended and may identify problems before they become expensive.

Pay attention to:

- Engine oil and other fluids recommended by the manufacturer.
- Air filters and scheduled replacements.
- Wheel alignment if the vehicle pulls to one side or tires wear unevenly.
- Spark plugs and engine maintenance where applicable.
- Tire condition and pressure.
- Warning lights and unusual engine behaviour.

Do not replace parts unnecessarily just because someone claims they will improve mileage. Follow your vehicle's maintenance schedule and get suspected problems checked by a qualified mechanic.

A well-maintained car is not only more likely to use fuel efficiently; it can also be safer and more reliable.

## 10. Compare Routes and Choose the Most Efficient Way to Travel

The shortest route is not always the route that uses the least fuel.

A short journey through heavy traffic, frequent signals, or roadworks may consume more fuel than a slightly longer route with smoother traffic.

Before leaving, check your navigation app for:

- Traffic congestion.
- Road closures and construction.
- Alternative routes.
- Expected journey times.
- Opportunities to combine nearby stops.

You can also consider walking, cycling, or public transportation for suitable short journeys.

If you regularly travel the same route, compare your fuel consumption over time to see which practical changes work best for your vehicle.

## Bonus Tip: Track Your Fuel Expenses

You cannot easily identify your biggest fuel expenses if you never track them.

For a few weeks, record your fuel purchases, approximate distance driven, and major journeys.

You can then calculate your fuel economy and compare it over time.

For example, if your car travels 400 miles using 16 gallons of fuel, its fuel economy is:

**400 ÷ 16 = 25 miles per gallon (MPG).**

For metric measurements, if your car travels 600 kilometres using 40 litres of fuel, its consumption is:

**(40 ÷ 600) × 100 = 6.67 litres per 100 kilometres.**

Use consistent measurement methods and compare similar types of driving. Traffic, weather, passengers, air-conditioning use, and road conditions can all affect your results.

## How Much Money Can You Save by Following These Tips?

Your savings depend on how much you drive, your vehicle's fuel efficiency, local fuel prices, and your current driving habits.

For example, a driver spending $200 a month on fuel who reduces that expense by 10% would save $20 per month, or $240 over a year.

This is an illustrative calculation, not a guarantee that every driver will achieve a 10% reduction.

You may save more by combining several changes, such as maintaining correct tire pressure, reducing unnecessary journeys, driving smoothly, and sharing regular commutes.

Carpooling can be particularly useful because it can reduce how much fuel cost you personally bear, even when the vehicle's total fuel consumption remains similar.

## Final Thoughts

Saving money on gas doesn't always require a major lifestyle change. It can begin with checking your tire pressure, removing unnecessary weight, driving smoothly, planning your errands, and maintaining your vehicle.

However, you don't have to solve every fuel expense through better driving alone. Sometimes, the smarter approach is to share the journey.
