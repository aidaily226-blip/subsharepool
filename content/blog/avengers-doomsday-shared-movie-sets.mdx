# Do Movie Studios Share Sets? How Avengers: Doomsday Reused Marvel's Baxter Building

When you watch a huge Hollywood movie, it can feel like every location you see was created specifically for that film.

A futuristic laboratory.

A superhero headquarters.

A spaceship interior.

A giant apartment.

A city street.

But Hollywood doesn't always build everything from scratch.

Movie studios can **reuse, modify and share sets** between productions. Sometimes an expensive set built for one movie becomes part of another movie months or even years later.

Marvel has a particularly interesting recent example.

The sets created for **The Fantastic Four: First Steps** were also designed with **Avengers: Doomsday** in mind.

That means the same physical production environment could serve two different Marvel movies — with changes made to suit the new story.

This raises an interesting question:

**Are movie studios actually sharing their sets?**

The answer is yes.

---

## What Does It Mean to Reuse a Movie Set?

A movie set is a physical environment constructed or prepared for filming.

It could be:

- A house
- An office
- A laboratory
- A spaceship
- A restaurant
- A superhero headquarters
- A city street
- A castle
- A hospital
- A fictional building

Instead of destroying a set after one production finishes, filmmakers can sometimes keep it and modify it for another production.

This process can involve changing:

- Furniture
- Walls
- Doors
- Windows
- Lighting
- Paint
- Decorations
- Signs
- Props
- Computer screens
- Backgrounds

The basic structure can remain while the appearance and purpose of the set change.

This is often called **set redressing**.

In simple terms:

> The studio keeps the expensive structure and changes what the audience sees.

---

# Avengers: Doomsday Is a Great Example

One of the most interesting examples comes from Marvel's **Avengers: Doomsday**.

The film follows **The Fantastic Four: First Steps**, which introduced a completely different version of the Marvel universe and its futuristic 1960s-inspired Baxter Building.

Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige confirmed that the production team designed the Baxter Building sets for **both movies**.

That means the sets weren't created exclusively for *The Fantastic Four: First Steps*.

They were also intended to be used when making *Avengers: Doomsday*.

And that makes sense from a production perspective.

Why build the same environment twice?

If a major set already exists and fits the story of another movie, the production can reuse it and make the necessary changes.

---

## The Baxter Building Wasn't Just a Digital Image

One reason this is particularly interesting is that the Baxter Building wasn't simply a computer-generated location.

*The Fantastic Four: First Steps* used substantial physical production design.

The filmmakers built environments that actors could actually walk through, touch and interact with.

The Baxter Building included spaces such as:

- The Fantastic Four's living areas
- Laboratories
- Workspaces
- Corridors
- Other interior environments

Those physical environments could then become valuable production assets for another movie.

Instead of starting from an empty soundstage, another production can begin with something that has already been designed and constructed.

---

# Why Would Marvel Share a Movie Set?

The biggest reason is simple:

**Building physical sets takes time and money.**

A large movie set requires:

1. Production designers
2. Construction teams
3. Carpenters
4. Painters
5. Prop departments
6. Lighting teams
7. Set decorators
8. Art departments
9. Studio space

A complicated environment can take weeks or months to design and construct.

If another Marvel movie needs the same location, reusing the existing structure can be much more practical than rebuilding it from zero.

This doesn't mean the second movie simply walks onto the exact same untouched set.

The production may modify it significantly.

But the underlying structure can still be reused.

---

# It's Not Only About Saving Money

Cost is obviously important, but set reuse can provide another advantage:

**Consistency.**

Imagine that the Fantastic Four's headquarters appears in two different movies.

If Marvel rebuilt the location from scratch for every movie, the designers would have to recreate the architecture, proportions and visual details.

By reusing the original set, filmmakers can maintain continuity.

The audience can recognize:

> "This is the same place."

That makes fictional worlds feel more connected.

For a universe as interconnected as the Marvel Cinematic Universe, that can be extremely valuable.

---

# Avengers: Doomsday Is Also Using Many Practical Sets

Another interesting aspect of *Avengers: Doomsday* is its approach to physical filmmaking.

Production designer **Gavin Bocquet** has said that approximately **80% of the interior sets with backings used physical or painted backdrops**, rather than automatically relying on blue screens and replacing the environment digitally.

The goal was to create more of the environment physically whenever practical.

That doesn't mean *Doomsday* contains no CGI.

A movie involving superheroes, alternate realities and enormous action sequences obviously requires extensive visual effects.

But the production team wanted to use physical environments where they made sense.

---

# What Is a Practical Movie Set?

A practical set is something that physically exists on the filming location.

For example, an actor might actually be standing inside:

**A real room + real walls + real furniture + real doors**

rather than standing in an empty studio with everything added digitally later.

This can give actors something tangible to interact with.

It can also affect:

- Lighting
- Shadows
- Reflections
- Camera movement
- Actor performances
- The overall appearance of the shot

Visual effects can then be added later when necessary.

---

# Can the Same Set Become a Completely Different Location?

Yes.

This is one of the most fascinating things about filmmaking.

A production designer can take the same basic environment and make it look completely different.

For example:

### Version 1

Modern office.

### Version 2

1960s laboratory.

### Version 3

Alien spaceship.

### Version 4

Government headquarters.

The walls might remain similar, but everything around them can change.

Furniture, colors, signs, props, lighting and camera angles can completely alter how the audience interprets the environment.

This is why two movies can sometimes look as though they were filmed in completely different places even when they used the same underlying set.

---

# Do Different Movies Share the Same Studio?

Yes.

There's another form of sharing that happens throughout Hollywood.

A **soundstage** is a large controlled indoor space where productions can construct sets and film scenes.

The same studio complex can host different productions at different times.

For example:

**Production A**

↓  

Uses Soundstage 1

↓  

Finishes filming

↓  

Set is removed or modified

↓

**Production B**

↓  

Uses Soundstage 1

This isn't unusual.

Large studios have limited amounts of specialized production space, so using that infrastructure efficiently is an important part of filmmaking.

# Hollywood Has Been Reusing Sets for Decades

The idea isn't new.

Long before today's superhero movies, Hollywood productions regularly reused:

- Streets
- Houses
- Buildings
- Restaurants
- Offices
- Western towns
- Courtrooms
- Police stations
- Hospital interiors

Studio backlots were particularly useful.

A backlot could contain permanent streets and buildings that could represent completely different places depending on how they were filmed and dressed.

One street could become New York in one production and another fictional city in another.

The audience doesn't necessarily know they're looking at the same physical location.

---

# Why Don't Studios Just Build Everything Digitally?

Modern visual effects make it possible to create almost anything.

So why spend money building physical sets?

Because physical environments still have advantages.

Actors can interact with real objects.

Cameras can move through real spaces.

Lighting interacts naturally with physical surfaces.

Props produce real shadows and reflections.

And actors don't have to imagine everything around them.

For some productions, a combination works best:

**Physical set + practical props + digital extensions + CGI**

This hybrid approach allows filmmakers to get the advantages of both physical and digital filmmaking.

# Movie Set Sharing Is Similar to Other Forms of Resource Sharing

This is where the idea becomes bigger than Marvel.

Think about what happens when a studio builds an expensive set.

The studio has essentially created a **resource**.

That resource doesn't necessarily have to be used once.

It can potentially be:

- Reused
- Modified
- Shared
- Repurposed
- Stored
- Used by another production

The same basic idea exists in many industries.

Companies share offices.

Businesses share equipment.

People share transportation.

Creators share audiences and platforms.

And movie productions can reuse expensive physical production resources.

# What Happens to a Movie Set After Filming?

There isn't one universal answer.

Depending on the production, a set might be:

### 1. Destroyed

Some sets are temporary and are dismantled after filming.

### 2. Stored

Important components may be stored for possible future use.

### 3. Modified

The set can be changed for another scene or production.

### 4. Reused

Another movie or television production can use the same environment.

### 5. Kept as a Permanent Backlot

Some studio environments can remain available for future productions.

The decision depends on the cost, storage requirements, studio space and likelihood of future use.

# Could Avengers: Doomsday Use the Same Sets Again?

Potentially, yes.

Marvel is making interconnected stories where characters, locations and fictional worlds can appear across multiple productions.

If a physical environment is useful for another project, keeping or adapting it can make sense.

However, it is important not to assume that every Marvel set will automatically appear in another movie.

Some sets are created for a specific production and are eventually dismantled.

The key point is that **set reuse is a legitimate and established filmmaking practice**, and Marvel has explicitly used this approach with the Baxter Building.

# So, Are Marvel Movies Really Sharing Sets?

**Yes — in the production sense.**

Marvel has confirmed that the Baxter Building sets created for *The Fantastic Four: First Steps* were also built for use in *Avengers: Doomsday*.

The sets were not necessarily identical forever; they could be updated and adapted for the new production.

At the same time, *Doomsday* is using a significant amount of practical production design, with physical backings used across many interior sets.

So while audiences see a completely new Marvel adventure on screen, some of the physical filmmaking infrastructure behind it can be reused.

That's one of the hidden ways Hollywood works.

# FAQ

## Does Avengers: Doomsday reuse sets from Fantastic Four?

Yes. Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige confirmed that the Baxter Building sets were designed for both *The Fantastic Four: First Steps* and *Avengers: Doomsday*.

## What is set redressing?

Set redressing means changing an existing movie set using new furniture, props, decorations, paint, signs or other elements so that it can represent a different scene or location.

## Do movie studios reuse sets?

Yes. Movie studios have reused and modified sets for decades. Permanent backlots, soundstages and physical sets can all be reused depending on the production.

## Does Avengers: Doomsday use real sets?

Yes. Production designer Gavin Bocquet has described the use of physical sets and backings across a large portion of the film's interior environments.

## Why do movies reuse sets?

Reusing sets can reduce construction work, save time and money, maintain visual continuity and allow production teams to focus resources on new parts of the story.

## Are Marvel movie environments all CGI?

No. Marvel productions use a mixture of practical sets, physical props, locations, digital environments and visual effects.

## Can the same movie set appear in different movies?

Yes. The same physical set can be reused or redesigned for different productions. Sometimes the changes are so extensive that audiences don't realize they're looking at the same underlying environment.

A set may have been redesigned.

A prop may have been stored and reused.

And in the case of Marvel's **Avengers: Doomsday**, one of the most recognizable fictional locations — the **Baxter Building** — was designed to serve more than one movie.
